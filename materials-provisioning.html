AT A GLANCE

One workspace replaces a five-spreadsheet manual process.

  • Quadrant filter — one tap narrows the list to the price/risk category being worked through.
  • Historical usage — attached to every row, so past demand is never a separate lookup.
  • Recommended quantity — the app's calculated suggestion sits right next to the part it applies to.
THE CHALLENGE

Five spreadsheets, one guessing game on high-value parts.

WHAT PLANNERS WERE DEALING WITH

  • Spreadsheet fatigue: every decision meant cross-referencing up to five Excel files, plus vendor PDFs and email.
  • Buried deals: vendor "push lists" for used parts sat in inboxes, so cheaper USM alternatives routinely got missed.
  • Guesswork on high-value parts: no forecasting tool meant planners defaulted to over-ordering out of fear of shopfloor delays.

WHAT THE APP DOES INSTEAD

  • One matrix, one workspace: a 2×2 grid (price × usage likelihood) drives both layout and purchasing logic.
  • Guardrails, not spreadsheets: live budget bars and a predictive formula handle the math planners used to do by hand.
  • USM in context: vendor market lists sit inline, next to the part they apply to.

THE SOLUTION · FEATURE 01

Categorizing Parts by Price & Risk

UX rationale — stakeholders had already defined the rules for each quadrant; turning them into a visible 2×2 grid let Elena see at a glance which parts needed a quick lookup versus a full forecast, instead of memorizing four policies.

  • Quadrant tag — every part sits in one of four categories; the tag determines which purchasing rule applies automatically.
THE SOLUTION · FEATURE 02

Coverage Tracking

UX rationale — without this view, it's easy to accidentally double-order a part that's already partially fulfilled. One progress bar lets Elena see fulfillment status at a glance instead of cross-checking manually.

  • Coverage progress bar — shows how much of the part's demand is covered by new stock, USM, and incoming shipments.
  • Recommended quantity — the remaining amount to buy, calculated automatically.
THE SOLUTION · FEATURE 03

Vendor USM Matchmaker Panel

UX rationale — these deals used to arrive as static PDFs, disconnected from the part they applied to. Sliding the data next to the line item turned a passive email into an active, in-context decision.

  • Vendor comparison — price and condition side by side across every matching vendor.
  • Cost savings — a running total shows exactly what's saved versus ordering new.
THE SOLUTION · FEATURE 04

Submission Cart & Review Queue

UX rationale — staging picks in a cart before submission means Elena can review everything at once instead of submitting parts one at a time.

  • Staged parts — every pick sits in a review queue before going out as an order, catching mistakes before submission.
THE SOLUTION · FEATURE 05

Approval Monitoring

UX rationale — every submitted order needs a single place to check status, rather than following up over email.

  • Status tracking — every submitted order tracked by status, planner, and the engine/customer it's tied to.

REFLECTIONS

Key UX Takeaways

  1. 01Business rules should disappear into the UI. A visible matrix and pre-filled defaults let planners follow complex policy without consciously thinking about it.
  2. 02Show the math, don't hide it. An auto-calculated recommendation is only trustworthy if the planner can check it on hover.
  3. 03External data is only useful where the decision happens. Vendor push lists had real value going to waste in an inbox — pulling that data into the line item made it actionable.
