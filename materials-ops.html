AT A GLANCE

One live workspace replaces the shortage spreadsheet.

  • 1Engine / Parts toggle — one tap switches whether shortages group by engine or by part.
  • 2Live stock chips — GV, GCDR and GHR counts sit directly against demand — no more cross-checking spreadsheets.
  • 3Add / Add Alt — a blue icon flags approved substitutes; one click adds either to the cart.
  • 4Shared Approval Cart — live count of items staged for sign-off, visible to the whole team.
THE CHALLENGE

Two things were costing shopfloor time.

WHAT PLANNERS WERE DEALING WITH

  • Spreadsheet fatigue: cross-referencing shortages against stock meant switching between multiple static Excel files.
  • Email bottlenecks: requisition approvals ran through manual threads — easy to lose track of, and it held up engine assembly.
  • Hidden substitutes: even when an approved alternative part existed, finding it quickly wasn't easy.

WHAT THE APP DOES INSTEAD

  • One view for stock: real-time counts sit directly against part demand.
  • Blue alternative rows: an icon flags when alternatives exist, expanding into nested rows on demand.
  • Two fulfillment modes: toggle between clearing one Engine or batch-fulfilling one part across every engine that needs it.

THE SOLUTION · FEATURE 02

Keeping Context When Entering Orders

UX rationale — a slide-out drawer instead of a modal or a new page: a modal blocks the table behind it, a page reload loses your scroll position. The drawer lets planners adjust an order while the row they're working on stays visible.

  • 1Live fulfillment % — updates in real time as stock is added to the cart, so a planner never double-orders.
  • 2Vendor comparison — price, MOQ and lead time side by side across NEW and USM sources, sortable.
  • 3Quantity → extended price — catalog constraints (MOQ/UPQ) are enforced before the order can be submitted.
THE SOLUTION · FEATURE 03

Replacing Email Approvals with a Shared Cart

UX rationale — email threads meant no one had a single, current view of what was pending. A shared cart gives the manager full context on a part and lets them sign off in one tap: In Cart → Pending PPCS approval → PPCS Approved → ePR Approved.

  • 1Live collaboration — planner and manager see the same cart update in real time.
  • 2Review queue — every staged part shows its status at a glance — complete, pending, incomplete.
  • 3One-tap submit — the manager signs off without digging through an email thread.

REFLECTIONS

Key UX Takeaways

  1. 01Dense tables need a clear hierarchy. Color-coded stock chips and slide-out drawers kept the data scannable without losing the planner's place.
  2. 02Progressive disclosure beats always-on clutter. Flagging substitutes with an icon let planners explore alternatives only when they needed to.
  3. 03One user, two jobs. A one-click switch between Engine and Part views let the same planner do both without separate tools.
